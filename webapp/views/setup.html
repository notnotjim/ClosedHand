  1. 01

    ClosedHand keeps what it learns about you, its index of your mail and your settings in a database that belongs to you. It arrives with the install and starts on its own, so this step usually lights up before you ever see it.

    Not lit yet? The database can take a minute on first boot. If it stays dark, something went wrong with the install itself; re-running the installer is the quickest fix.

  2. 02

    You can add more models later and choose which one handles each of ClosedHand's jobs, in Settings.

    ClosedHand identifies the provider from the key itself.

  3. 03

    The dashboard holds everything ClosedHand knows about you. There is no username, because this instance has exactly one owner.

  4. 04

    ClosedHand needs to read your Gmail, Calendar and Drive. Because it runs on your machine and not through a company, you grant that access yourself by creating a Google dev project (don't worry, it's not as complicated as it sounds).

    Connected. ClosedHand reads this account's mail, calendar and Drive so it can recall them, and changes nothing unless you ask. Other Google accounts can be added later from the dashboard.

    Manage in the dashboard Connect a different account
    Step 1 of 5

    Each step opens Google in a new tab. Do the step, close that tab, come back here.

    Create your project

    Google keeps the access in a free project that only you can use and can delete later. Click the button below and name the project anything you like. Leave the second field as 'No Organization'.

    Important: before you click Create on the project screen, copy the Project ID shown under the name you chose (often the name itself, sometimes with numbers added). This is what goes into the box below.

    Open the New Project screen Where the ID is

    Switch on the services

    One screen enables Gmail, Calendar and Drive together. Click the button below and on the screen that appears, click Next, then Enable. Once the permissions are enabled, come back here and move to the next step.

    Switch on the three services

    Introduce the app

    Fill out the form by clicking the button below and putting the following in each field:

    1. App name: ClosedHand
    2. User support email: choose your email address from the dropdown
    3. Audience: External. Nothing is shared with anyone by this: it is Google's word for an app that ordinary Google accounts like yours can sign in to, rather than one locked to a company's workspace.
    4. Contact information: your email address
    5. Finish: tick to agree, then click Create

    Then come back here and move to the next step.

    Open the form

    Make the sign-in key

    Open the Clients page below and click + Create client. Choose Web application from the dropdown (you can leave the Name field as is). Under Authorized redirect URIs, paste this line exactly:

    http://localhost:3000/auth/google/callback
    Open the Clients page

    Important: press Create, then click Download JSON before closing the dialog. Google shows that file only once; if you miss it, create another client.

    Where Download JSON is

    Then hand ClosedHand the file you downloaded. Pick it here, or drop it on the box, or open it and paste the contents.

    Publish the app and sign in

    While the app stays in "Testing", Google makes you sign in again every seven days, so it needs publishing. Open the Branding page below, scroll down to App domain, and fill in:

    1. Application home page: https://closedhand.com
    2. Application privacy policy link: https://closedhand.com/privacy
    3. Authorized domains: click + Add domain and type closedhand.com
    4. Click Save
    Open the Branding page

    Then open the Audience page below, click Publish app and Confirm. The "Push to production?" box is Google's standard wording for any published app; no verification is needed.

    Open the Audience page

    Last thing: sign in. Google will warn that the app is unverified, because it is your private one. Choose Advanced, then Go to ClosedHand, then Continue. On the permissions screen, tick every box (there is a Select all), then Continue. ClosedHand needs all of them.

    Sign in with Google

    The sign-in button appears once the file from step four is saved.

    Telegram connected

    A separate bot identity instead of your own number. Three taps in BotFather.

    1. Scan to open @BotFather, or tap it if you're on this device.
    2. Send /newbot and answer its two questions.
    3. Paste the token it gives you below.

    Telegram has no way to create a bot for you, so this part is yours. It takes about a minute.

    Scan this, then send your bot any message. That locks it to your Telegram account: it answers you and refuses everyone else.

    On this device instead? Open your bot.

    Note you can also connect Discord, Slack, LINE and the web chat any time from the dashboard.
