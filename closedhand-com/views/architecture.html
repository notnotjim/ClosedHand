How ClosedHand works

Your LLM starts with the context.

A useful answer can depend on something you never thought to mention. ClosedHand brings relevant details from across your connected sources into the conversation, including connections the LLM might never have chosen to search.

Your information is ready before the question

ClosedHand keeps useful information from supported mail, calendars, files and other connected sources together on the computer running it. Regular updates prepare that information before you ask, so each conversation can draw on it without starting a new search in every app.

When you send a message, ClosedHand recalls by meaning, not just keywords, ranks the matches and supplies the relevant parts to your chosen LLM. The information lives with ClosedHand. Changing your LLM does not mean starting your memory again.

Connections can reveal things you did not ask about

Ask about a delayed flight and the useful answer may involve a dinner in your calendar, a pickup in your trip plan and a change you discussed last week. Holding those sources together helps ClosedHand connect the details and suggest what needs your attention.

This is preemptive awareness: preparing useful context before the LLM decides which tools to use. Pulse can also notice things worth bringing to your attention and message you through your connected chat apps.

Live tools still have a job

Fresh flight status, weather, purchases and other actions still need live tools. Connections share a common way to describe information worth keeping; permissions and what each service makes available determine coverage. Recent updates, missing history and retrieval limits still matter. No system can promise perfect recall.

You choose the boundaries

Run ClosedHand on your own computer or server. Choose hosted or local models, inspect and edit what it remembers, and grant access to your real computer separately from its sandbox Workspace. Hosted models receive the content needed for their work.

The software is fully open source under Apache 2.0. You can inspect how it works and keep using it without committing your memory to one model provider.
